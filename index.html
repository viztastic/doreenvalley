Reason one

Noise

What makes the noise

The developers' own words: the main noise is “the inverters and cooling fans required to regulate battery cell temperature.” They run whenever the batteries work, which is most of the time.

Why it is worse here

The houses are above the site and facing it, across open paddocks. There is almost no background noise after dark to cover a steady hum.

Why “within limits” is not enough

Fans and inverters make a low, droning sound, and EPA Victoria treats that character as more disturbing than the same loudness without it. EPA also says compliance with the limits does not settle the question:

Even where a person complies with the prescribed noise limits, that noise could still be found to be unreasonable — relevant where the impact “is not well represented by the assessment method in EPA publication 1826”, expressly naming low frequency noise.EPA Victoria, Legislative noise framework

An objection can also rely on section 166 of the Environment Protection Act 2017, the general duty in section 25, regulation 120 on noise frequency, and EPA publication 1996 on low-frequency noise.

What to ask for

  • The expected noise level at each affected house, for daytime, evening and night, published before the decision is made.
  • Existing noise levels measured in this valley at night, not assumed from a general rural figure.
  • An assessment of the low, droning character of the noise, not just an average reading.
  • Permit conditions that limit noise measured at the house, checked after it is built, with something done about it if it fails.
  • Noise walls or enclosures promised in the permit, not left as a possibility.
Reason two

The view

What would actually be built

Council's own description: a utility-scale battery, a 220 kV terminal station, underground electrical infrastructure, water storage and firefighting infrastructure, access tracks, buildings and security fencing. A terminal station is a substation — steel structures, gantries and transformers.

Zoned to prevent exactly this

The whole site is Green Wedge, the zoning that keeps industrial development out of Melbourne's rural landscapes. Part of it also carries an Environmental Significance Overlay.

The screening does not work from above

The only mitigation published is landscaping “on site boundaries to help screen the BESS from view.” No heights, no setbacks and no images from elevated homes have been released, so nobody can test it.

And it is close to permanent

Expected to run until about 2050, with removal left to a permit condition written later — while the developers acknowledge there is no commercial-scale recycling for these units yet.

What to ask for

  • Photographs or montages showing the finished plant as seen from houses above the valley, not from the road at the fence.
  • Maximum heights for every structure, including the terminal station and any noise wall.
  • An assessment against the purpose of the Green Wedge Zone, not only the energy case.
  • A funded plan, with money set aside, to remove the plant and restore the land — agreed before approval.

Two further concerns

Nobody can appeal the decision

The Minister decides under a provision called Clause 53.22, leaving the Council able only to comment. Council puts it plainly: “There are no third-party appeal rights for either Council or the community in relation to the Minister's decision under this process.”

No VCAT hearing afterwards. The objection period is the only step.

Fire risk

The site is in a designated Bushfire Prone Area, though per Council it is not under a Bushfire Management Overlay. The developers concede that “while fires associated with BESS technology are rare, there are risks and hazards that need to be managed.”

Their fire assessment follows the right CFA guidelines — but it has not been published, and no water supply or separation figures are on the record.

What we are not saying

Victoria needs battery storage as its coal stations close, and we do not dispute that. Our objection is to this location, this design, and the missing figures.
